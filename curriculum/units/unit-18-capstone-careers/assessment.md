# Unit 18 Assessment — Capstone, Presentations & Careers/Certifications

> This unit's assessment is **mostly performance-based**: the capstone report and presentation are the main grades. A short careers quiz, the careers research mini-task, and a final course reflection round it out.

## Formative checks
- **Exit tickets / status checks** (Days 1–5): five lifecycle phases in order; scanning vs enumeration + a tool for each; one Linux privesc check; daily phase-status line; one career path + one certification + an honest next step.
- **Capstone checklist milestones:** instructor initials each completed lifecycle phase.
- **Evidence spot-check:** instructor verifies labeled screenshots are tied to exact commands.

## Quiz (short careers & ethics check)

1. Put the attack lifecycle in the correct order:
   - A) Exploit → recon → document → scan
   - B) Recon → scan/enumerate → exploit → privilege-escalate → document
   - C) Document → exploit → recon → scan
   - D) Scan → document → recon → exploit

2. A **SOC analyst** primarily works on which side of security?
   - A) Offense (red team)  B) Defense — monitoring and responding to threats  C) Sales  D) Hardware repair

3. **GRC** stands for:
   - A) Global Routing Control  B) Governance, Risk, and Compliance  C) Graphics Rendering Card  D) General Recon Command

4. Which is a common **first** certification for someone entering cybersecurity?
   - A) OSCP  B) Security+  C) PhD  D) CISSP

5. **OSCP** is best described as:
   - A) A beginner multiple-choice exam
   - B) A hands-on offensive-security certification (the professional version of this course's skills)
   - C) A programming language
   - D) A type of firewall

6. A reasonable certification **roadmap** for offensive security is:
   - A) OSCP → Security+ → nothing
   - B) Security+ → eJPT/PNPT → OSCP and beyond
   - C) Random order, certifications don't build on each other
   - D) You must get all certifications at once

7. **CyberSeek** is a tool that helps you:
   - A) Hack faster
   - B) Explore cybersecurity job roles, demand, and career pathways
   - C) Bypass firewalls
   - D) Write exploits

8. Which is a legal way to **keep practicing** offensive skills after this class?
   - A) Test your neighbor's Wi-Fi without asking
   - B) Scan random websites "just to learn"
   - C) picoCTF, authorized CTFs, and your own home lab
   - D) Any system, as long as you change nothing

9. The **only** thing that separates a penetration tester from a criminal is:
   - A) Better tools  B) Written authorization and a defined scope  C) A college degree  D) A faster computer

10. A friend asks you to "just check if my ex's account can be hacked." The correct response is:
    - A) Do it quickly since it's small
    - B) Refuse — you have no authorization; it's illegal and could end your career
    - C) Do it but don't change anything
    - D) Ask for payment first

11. In your capstone, you got stuck and captured fewer flags than another team. For your grade, what matters most?
    - A) Only the number of flags
    - B) A clear, honest, well-documented report and presentation of what you actually did
    - C) Making up extra findings to look better
    - D) Nothing — you failed

12. The **presentation rubric** specifically requires you to address:
    - A) Your favorite tool  B) Authorization and the impact of your findings  C) Your GPA  D) The weather

## Performance task 1 — Capstone penetration-test report
**Prompt:** As a team, document your full-lifecycle test of the **approved capstone target** and produce a complete penetration-test report (executive summary, methodology, findings with evidence/justified severity/impact, remediation, appendices). Restate scope/authorization. Report only what actually happened.

**Deliverable:** The complete capstone report.

**Rubric:** (penetration-test report rubric, `instructor/grading-and-rubrics.md`)
| Criteria | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|----------|---------------|----------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Executive summary** | Clear, non-technical, accurate risk framing | Mostly clear | Too technical/vague | Missing |
| **Methodology** | Phases clearly described and justified | Phases described | Partial | Missing |
| **Findings** | Each finding has evidence, severity, and impact | Most findings complete | Findings incomplete | Findings missing |
| **Remediation** | Specific, actionable fixes for each finding | General fixes | Vague fixes | Missing |
| **Communication** | Polished, well-organized, correct terminology | Solid | Rough | Unclear |

## Performance task 2 — Capstone presentation
**Prompt:** Deliver a 3–5 minute team presentation: target & scope, methodology, top findings with severity, remediation, and an explicit authorization/impact statement.

**Deliverable:** The live (or recorded) presentation + 3–5 slides.

**Rubric:** (presentation rubric, `instructor/grading-and-rubrics.md`)
| Criteria | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|----------|---------------|----------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Content accuracy** | Technically correct & complete | Minor errors | Several errors | Inaccurate |
| **Clarity & delivery** | Engaging, clear, well-paced | Clear | Hard to follow | Unprepared |
| **Ethics framing** | Explicitly addresses authorization/impact | Mentions ethics | Minimal | Absent |

## Mini-task — Careers research
**Prompt:** Using **CyberSeek** (cyberseek.org) and the **NICE Framework**, choose one cybersecurity role that interests you and produce a short (½–1 page) brief covering:
1. The **role** and what it does day-to-day (e.g., pentester, SOC analyst, red/blue team, GRC, AppSec).
2. **Typical skills** required and one **NICE work role** it maps to.
3. **Demand and pay** info from CyberSeek (general — openings/regional demand, salary range).
4. **One certification** that supports this path and where it fits on the Security+ → eJPT/PNPT → OSCP roadmap.
5. **Your honest next step** after this class (a competition like picoCTF/NCL/CyberPatriot, a home-lab project, a cert to study toward, or a course/college path).

**Deliverable:** The ½–1 page career brief.

**Rubric:**
| Criteria | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|----------|---------------|----------------|----------------|---------------|
| Role understanding | Accurate, specific day-to-day + NICE mapping | Mostly accurate | Vague | Missing/incorrect |
| Evidence from tools | Uses CyberSeek/NICE data correctly | Some data | Little data | None |
| Pathway & next step | Realistic cert placement + concrete personal next step | General plan | Vague | Missing |

## Final course reflection
**Prompt:** In ½–1 page, reflect on the semester:
- What is one skill you are proud of learning, and one thing that was hard?
- Restate, in your own words, the course's central ethics rule and one example of how you'll apply it for life.
- What will you do next to keep learning **legally**?

**Deliverable:** The reflection (graded for completeness/thoughtfulness and a clear, correct ethics statement).
