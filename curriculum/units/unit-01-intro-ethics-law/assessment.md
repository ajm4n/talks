# Unit 01 Assessment — Ethics, Law & the Hacker Mindset

## Formative checks
- **Daily exit tickets** (one per day, from the lesson plan).
- **Hat-sorting half-sheet** (Day 2): students label actor descriptions white/gray/black hat.
- **Scope-reading check** (Day 3): students correctly identify in-scope vs. out-of-scope items.
- **Journal-setup check** (end of Day 1/Day 5): page 1 has the safety reminder; baseline entry present.

## Quiz

1. What is the BEST description of offensive security?
   - A) Breaking into systems for fun
   - B) Legally attacking systems with permission to find weaknesses before criminals do
   - C) Writing antivirus software
   - D) Reporting people who hack

2. "You can't protect what you don't understand" is used in this course to explain why:
   - A) Defenders should learn how attackers think
   - B) Everyone should hack their school
   - C) Laws don't apply to security work
   - D) Antivirus is unnecessary

3. Which hacker acts **without** permission but usually claims good intentions — and is still acting illegally?
   - A) White hat   B) Blue hat   C) Gray hat   D) Red hat

4. In a security exercise, the **blue team** is the group that:
   - A) Simulates attackers   B) Writes the law   C) Defends, detects, and responds   D) Pays the bug bounties

5. The Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) primarily prohibits:
   - A) Owning hacking tools
   - B) Accessing a computer "without authorization" or "exceeding authorized access"
   - C) Learning about cybersecurity
   - D) Using Linux

6. True or False: Because they are minors, high-school students cannot get in legal trouble for unauthorized hacking.
   - A) True   B) False

7. A friend says, "Scanning a website I don't own is fine as long as I don't change anything." The best response is:
   - A) Correct, looking is always legal
   - B) Unauthorized scanning can still be illegal — "just looking" is not a defense
   - C) Only true on weekends
   - D) Only true if you use Kali

8. The single dividing line between a penetration tester and a criminal is:
   - A) How smart they are
   - B) Which tools they use
   - C) Written authorization and an agreed scope
   - D) Whether they wear a hat

9. You **accidentally** discover a real vulnerability in an app you use. Responsible disclosure means you should:
   - A) Exploit it to prove it's real, then post it online
   - B) Tell your friends so they can try it too
   - C) Not exploit or share it, and report it privately to the owner or a trusted adult
   - D) Ignore it completely and never tell anyone

10. A bug-bounty program is:
   - A) A contest to write the most bugs
   - B) A program where organizations pay ethical hackers to find and report vulnerabilities legally
   - C) A type of malware
   - D) A school club

11. "Tools are dual-use" means:
   - A) Each tool does two things
   - B) The same tools are used by professionals and criminals; owning them is generally legal, but **unauthorized use** is not
   - C) You need two tools for every job
   - D) Tools must be bought in pairs

12. The OffSec-style "Try Harder" mindset means:
   - A) Attack the system until it breaks, rules or not
   - B) Persist and problem-solve through productive struggle before giving up — within the rules
   - C) Skip the lab journal
   - D) Always ask for the answer first

13. **Short answer:** In 2–3 sentences, explain why the *same action* (for example, scanning a network) can be a legitimate job for one person and a crime for another.

14. **Short answer:** Give one example of curiosity channeled *ethically* and one of the same curiosity channeled *harmfully*.

## Project / performance task

**Prompt:** Complete the **Scenario Worksheet** (8 scenarios) from the lab. For each, decide legal/illegal and ethical/unethical, and write one sentence justifying each decision. Then complete the **signed ethics reflection**.

**Ethics reflection prompt:** "Why does authorization matter so much in this field? Describe one situation where you might be tempted to cross a line, and what you would do instead." (3–5 sentences, then sign the pledge.)

**Deliverable:** Completed Scenario Worksheet + signed ethics reflection + lab journal (page 1 safety reminder, baseline entry, role-play reflection).

**Rubric:**
| Criteria | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|----------|---------------|----------------|----------------|---------------|
| Legal/illegal classification | All scenarios correct with clear, accurate reasoning | Most correct with reasonable justification | Some correct; reasoning thin | Mostly incorrect or missing |
| Ethical reasoning | Thoughtfully distinguishes legal vs. ethical; nuanced | Distinguishes legal vs. ethical in most cases | Conflates the two; limited reasoning | Little or no ethical reasoning |
| Responsible-disclosure understanding | Clearly states do-not-exploit/share + report privately, with example | States the core rules correctly | Partial understanding | Misunderstands disclosure |
| Reflection & professionalism | Honest, specific reflection; pledge signed | Adequate reflection; pledge signed | Vague reflection | Incomplete or unsigned |
| Lab journal | Well-organized, complete, safety reminder present | Complete with minor gaps | Partially set up | Missing/not started |
