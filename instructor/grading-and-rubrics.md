# Grading & Rubrics (Instructor)

## Grade weighting

| Component | Weight |
|-----------|:------:|
| Lab completion & lab notebooks | 30% |
| Unit quizzes & checks for understanding | 20% |
| Projects (recon report, web-vuln writeup) | 25% |
| Capstone (CTF + report + presentation) | 20% |
| Professionalism & ethics (citizenship) | 5% |

## The lab journal
Every student keeps a **lab journal** (digital doc or notebook) for the whole semester. For each lab they record: date, objective, tools used, commands run, what happened (with screenshots where possible), findings, and a one-line reflection. This is the backbone of the "lab completion" grade and models real penetration-test note-taking. See the rubric below.

---

## Reusable rubrics

### Lab journal rubric (per lab, 12 pts)
| Criteria | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|----------|---------------|----------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Process documentation** | Every step, command, and result clearly recorded; reproducible | Most steps recorded | Some steps missing | Sparse/unclear |
| **Findings & analysis** | Accurately identifies & explains what was found and why it matters | Identifies findings with minor gaps | Findings listed without explanation | Findings missing/incorrect |
| **Professionalism** | Clean, organized, screenshots labeled, ethical notes present | Organized | Disorganized | Hard to follow |

### Penetration-test report rubric (projects & capstone, 20 pts)
| Criteria | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|----------|---------------|----------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Executive summary** | Clear, non-technical, accurate risk framing | Mostly clear | Too technical/vague | Missing |
| **Methodology** | Phases clearly described and justified | Phases described | Partial | Missing |
| **Findings** | Each finding has evidence, severity, and impact | Most findings complete | Findings incomplete | Findings missing |
| **Remediation** | Specific, actionable fixes for each finding | General fixes | Vague fixes | Missing |
| **Communication** | Polished, well-organized, correct terminology | Solid | Rough | Unclear |

### Presentation rubric (capstone, 12 pts)
| Criteria | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|----------|---------------|----------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Content accuracy** | Technically correct & complete | Minor errors | Several errors | Inaccurate |
| **Clarity & delivery** | Engaging, clear, well-paced | Clear | Hard to follow | Unprepared |
| **Ethics framing** | Explicitly addresses authorization/impact | Mentions ethics | Minimal | Absent |

### Professionalism & ethics (citizenship) rubric — ongoing
| Level | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Exemplary | Consistently models ethical behavior, helps peers, respects lab boundaries, reports issues responsibly |
| Proficient | Follows AUP, collaborates well |
| Developing | Occasional lapses in focus/boundaries |
| Concern | AUP/boundary violations — triggers conversation & possible escalation |

## Formative assessment ideas (low-stakes, frequent)
- **Exit tickets:** one question at the end of class ("What's the difference between passive and active recon?").
- **Command of the day:** student explains one command and its flags.
- **Think-pair-share** on ethics scenarios.
- **Lab checkpoints:** instructor initials a milestone mid-lab.
- **Flag submissions:** CTF flags as proof-of-completion.

## Academic integrity in a hacking class
Collaboration is encouraged in labs, but each student maintains their **own** lab journal and writes their **own** reports. Distinguish "getting help/pairing" (good) from "copying a writeup" (not). Walkthroughs are study aids; submitted work must reflect the student's own understanding — be ready to ask a student to explain any step they submitted.

## Re-tests & resets
Because labs can break, allow students to **reset VMs from snapshots** and re-attempt without penalty. Mastery matters more than first-try success in a skills course.
